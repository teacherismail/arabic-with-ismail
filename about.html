Who is Ismail

More Than a
Language Teacher

I was born in Egypt and lived in Jordan for 14 years — which means I speak both Egyptian and Jordanian Arabic fluently, and use them in everyday life. This unique background allows me to provide clear explanations, cultural insights, and real-life examples from different parts of the Arab world.

Before becoming a full-time Arabic teacher, I spent years hosting dialogue-based programs on YouTube, leading long, in-depth conversations about culture, society, history, and ideas across the Middle East. I interviewed thinkers, writers, researchers, and young people from different Arab countries.

That experience taught me how to listen carefully, ask meaningful questions, and appreciate the extraordinary diversity of perspectives that exist throughout the Arab world — skills that now shape every lesson I teach.

A Lifelong Love of Reading

The Books That
Shaped Me

Long before I became a teacher, I was an enthusiastic reader. As a teenager, I spent countless hours reading Arabic novels, detective stories, adventure series, biographies, and historical books.

Reading was not simply a hobby — it was one of the ways I discovered the world beyond my immediate surroundings. These books developed my curiosity, imagination, and love of storytelling — qualities that still influence how I teach today.

Because of this passion, I particularly enjoy helping students discover Arabic literature, novels, poetry, memoirs, and cultural texts as part of their language-learning journey.

المغامرون الخمسة
The Five Adventurers
By Mahmoud Salem

The beloved Egyptian detective series following five young detectives — Takhtakh, Loza, Atef, Moheb, and Nosa — and their loyal dog Zinger, solving mysteries across Egypt.

Inspired by Enid Blyton's "The Five Find-Outers" — but brilliantly recreated for Arab readers with Egyptian personalities, Cairo settings, and a uniquely Egyptian spirit.

Arabic Literary Tradition
Novels · Poetry · Memoirs · History

From Manfaluti's sentimental prose to Mahfouz's Cairo Trilogy — Ismail guides students through Arabic literature in the original language, making it accessible and alive.

My Journey Through the Arab World

Egypt & Jordan —
Two Worlds, One Teacher

Although Arabs share a language and many cultural traditions, each country has its own personality, history, customs, humor, and way of expressing ideas. Living in both Egypt and Jordan gave me a perspective few Arabic teachers can offer.

🇪🇬

Egypt — The Mother of the World

Born & Raised · Egyptian Arabic

Egypt's rich literary, artistic, and historical traditions run deep in everything I teach. Egyptian Arabic is the most widely understood dialect in the Arab world — spoken, sung, and watched by hundreds of millions. My Egyptian background keeps me intimately connected to this living tradition.

🇯🇴

Jordan — The Heart of the Levant

14 Years · Levantine Arabic

Living in Jordan for 14 years immersed me in Levantine culture, dialect, and society. I understand how Jordanians — and by extension, Palestinians, Syrians, and Lebanese — express themselves, think, and communicate. This gives my students access to both major Arabic dialect families.

"My years across the Arab world taught me that language is inseparable from culture. When I teach Arabic, I'm not just teaching words — I'm opening a window into how people live, think, and feel."

What Ismail Teaches

Three Forms of
Arabic Fluency

Arabic is not one language — it is a family of languages sharing a common soul. Ismail teaches all three major forms, helping students find the right Arabic for their specific goal.

العامية المصرية

Egyptian Arabic

The most widely understood Arabic dialect in the world — spoken in films, songs, and television watched across the Arab world. Perfect for anyone wanting to connect with the largest Arabic-speaking culture, from Cairo's streets to Hollywood scenes.

اللهجة الأردنية

Jordanian Arabic

The Levantine dialect family — spoken in Jordan, Palestine, Syria, and Lebanon. Ismail's 14 years in Jordan give him native-level fluency in this dialect, making it accessible, natural, and culturally rich for students targeting the Levant region.

الفصحى

Modern Standard Arabic

The formal, written Arabic used in news, literature, official communications, and international settings. Essential for diplomats, journalists, academics, lawyers, and anyone engaging with the Arab world at a professional or institutional level.

Teaching Philosophy

Language is
a Bridge,
Not a Subject.

My lessons are friendly, practical, and tailored to your goals. Whether you're a complete beginner or preparing for a specific professional context, my focus is always the same: helping you communicate naturally, confidently, and comfortably.

I believe students learn best when language is connected to real life, meaningful conversations, and topics they genuinely care about. That is why every lesson is personalized to your interests, goals, and learning style.

  • Every lesson is personalized — no two students follow the same path.
  • Culture is always part of the lesson — history, customs, literature, music, humor.
  • Real conversations, not textbook drills — Arabic as it is actually spoken.
  • Your goal drives everything — travel, work, film, diplomacy, reading, or curiosity.
  • Complex ideas made simple — grammar explained clearly, never overwhelmingly.
  • The Arab world is diverse — Ismail never reduces it to stereotypes or a single image.
  • Learning never truly ends — our conversation continues beyond every lesson.
