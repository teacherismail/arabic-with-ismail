Who is Ismail

Arabic is not just a
language. It's a world.

I'm Ismail — an Egyptian Arabic teacher, cultural guide, and literary enthusiast based in Cairo. For years I have helped professionals, artists, actors, and curious souls navigate the rich, layered world of the Arabic language.

My approach is entirely personal. I don't believe in textbooks alone. I believe in conversations, stories, songs, poems, and the living breath of the language as it is spoken on the streets of Cairo and written in the pages of Manfaluti.

Whether you need Egyptian dialect for a film scene, Modern Standard Arabic for UN negotiations, or classical Arabic to read 19th-century literature — I will take you there.

Arabic Literary Heritage

120 Years of Arabic Literature

From Manfaluti's tearful elegies to Naguib Mahfouz's Cairo Trilogy — Arabic literature of the modern era is one of humanity's greatest treasures. I guide students through these works in their original language.

1876 – 1924

Mustafa Lutfi Al-Manfaluti

The master of Arabic sentimental prose. We read Al-Nazarat and Al-Abarat together — texts that taught a generation what Arabic could feel like.

المنفلوطي
1911 – 2006

Naguib Mahfouz

Egypt's only Nobel Laureate. His Cairo Trilogy is a masterclass in colloquial-classical Arabic fusion and urban Egyptian storytelling.

نجيب محفوظ
1889 – 1931

Khalil Gibran

The Arab world's most translated author. Gibran's Arabic is lyrical, spiritual, and surprisingly accessible — a perfect bridge for learners.

جبران خليل جبران
Classical Poetry

Al-Mutanabbi & Classical Verse

The greatest Arabic poet who ever lived. His verse is memorized by Arabs to this day. Understanding him unlocks the soul of the language.

المتنبي
Modern Era

Taha Hussein & The Egyptian Renaissance

The blind genius who reshaped Arabic prose. Reading Al-Ayyam in Arabic is a rite of passage for serious students of the language.

طه حسين
Contemporary

Alaa Al-Aswany & New Cairo Voices

The Yacoubian Building brought Egyptian Arabic to the world stage. Reading him is reading modern Cairo — raw, complex, and alive.
